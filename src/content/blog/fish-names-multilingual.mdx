**True salmon** (Atlantic & Pacific species) are cold-water fish from Europe, North America, and Japan, famous for their river-to-sea migration. Their flesh ranges from pink to deep orange, rich in Omega-3, and varies from firm (King, Sockeye) to delicate (Coho, Pink). Wild Pacific salmon are prized for color and taste, while most Atlantic salmon available globally is farm-raised and milder.

**Atlantic Salmon** is almost entirely farmed in Norway, Scotland, and Canada. It has a mild, buttery flavour with soft pink flesh, perfect for grilling, baking, sushi, and smoking. Wild Atlantic salmon has become rare over time.

**Pacific Salmon** (Sockeye, King, Coho, Pink, Chum) are mostly wild-caught in Alaska, Canada, and Russia. They have deeper colour, firmer texture, and richer flavour. Sockeye is especially known for its intense red flesh.

**Indian Salmon[^9]** (Ravas, Gurjali, Kaana Meen, Kala Meen) is **called “salmon” only by name**. It is **not a true salmon**, but a warm-water **threadfin** species found along India’s **west coast**—especially Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka & Gujarat. Flesh is white to pale pink, soft, and mildly sweet, ideal for curries, steaming, and shallow frying.

**Trout in India:**
Trout[^6] belongs to the **same family as salmon (Salmonidae)** but is **not a sea fish**. It is a **freshwater cold-water fish**, originally introduced by the British. Rainbow Trout is found in Himalayan regions and also in the **Nilgiris—Ooty, Avalanche, and Kodaikanal**.

**Availability:**

 * **Indian Salmon (Ravas):** West-coast fish markets, metro seafood counters, online platforms.
 * **True Salmon (Atlantic/Pacific):** Mostly imported; available in **gourmet seafood counters**, premium supermarkets, hotels, and sushi restaurants.
 * **Trout:** Hill-state markets (Kashmir, Himachal, Uttarakhand) and **Nilgiris** (Ooty, Avalanche, Kodaikanal).

**Cold-water vs Warm-water Fish:**
True salmon grow in cold, oxygen-rich waters, giving firm, oily, colorful flesh. Indian salmon grows in warm tropical waters, resulting in softer, milder meat. Trout is freshwater, cold-water, and delicate in texture.

**Cooking Tips:**

 * **Atlantic/Pacific Salmon:** Best for grilling, baking, sushi, smoking.
 * **Indian Salmon (Ravas):** Excellent for curries, steaming, shallow frying.
 * **Trout:** Ideal for pan-frying or gentle grilling.

## ⚠️ FORMALIN AWARENESS - Critical Health Alert

:::danger[What is Formalin?]
Formalin is a **toxic chemical** (formaldehyde solution) illegally used to preserve fish. It's a **known carcinogen** that causes cancer, liver damage, kidney failure, and can be fatal.
:::

### 🚨 Why This Matters

Unethical vendors use formalin to make old fish look fresh and extend shelf life for profit. **This practice is illegal and extremely dangerous.**

### 🔍 How to Spot Formalin-Treated Fish

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ Unnaturally firm/rubbery texture (even after days)
- ⚠️ No fishy smell or chemical odor
- ⚠️ Looks "too fresh" after 2-3 days
- ⚠️ Powdery white coating on surface
- ⚠️ Eyes remain crystal clear (should cloud naturally)
- ⚠️ No flies or insects around the fish
- ⚠️ Skin irritation or burning when touched

**Quick Tests:**
- **Turmeric Test**: Apply turmeric paste; turns red/pink if formalin present
- **Water Test**: Soak in warm water; milky/cloudy water or pungent smell indicates formalin

### ✅ Stay Safe - Buy Smart

**Do:**
- ✓ Buy from trusted, licensed vendors
- ✓ Check natural freshness: clear eyes, red gills, mild ocean smell
- ✓ Prefer live fish or regulated markets
- ✓ Wash thoroughly before cooking

**Avoid:**
- ✗ Fish that looks "too perfect" after hours/days
- ✗ Fish with no smell whatsoever
- ✗ Vendors with poor hygiene
- ✗ Suspiciously cheap premium fish

### 📞 Report Contamination

**If you suspect formalin, DO NOT consume. Report immediately:**
- **FSSAI Helpline:** 1800-112-100
- **Consumer Helpline:** 1800-11-4000
- **Online:** [fssai.gov.in](https://fssai.gov.in/)

:::tip[Remember]
Your health matters more than saving money. Choose safety over price!
:::

:::note[About Ammonia Smell in Fish]
**Trimethylamine (TMA)** is a natural compound that causes the characteristic "fishy" or ammonia-like smell in seafood. When fish die, bacteria break down **TMAO** (trimethylamine oxide - naturally present in fish tissue) into **TMA**, producing that pungent odor. Fresh fish has minimal TMA, but as it ages, TMA levels increase. **Strong ammonia smell = old/spoiled fish**. Always choose fish with a mild, ocean-fresh scent, not a sharp chemical odor.
:::

## 📝 Usage Tips

:::tip[Shopping Guide]
- **Fresh Fish Indicators**: Clear eyes, bright red / pink gills, firm flesh, mild ocean / river smell
- **Market Names**: Regional names vary even within states - ask vendors for clarification
- **Seasonal Availability**: Some fish are seasonal - monsoon affects availability
:::


:::note[Cooking Notes]
- **Sea Fish**: Generally has a stronger flavor, best for grilling, frying, and curries
- **River Fish**: Milder taste, often preferred for steaming and light preparations
- **Storage**: Fresh fish keeps 1-2 days refrigerated (at 0 - 4°C), 2-3 months frozen (at -18°C); And Don't refreeze once thawed
- **Pre-cooking wash**: Always wash fish thoroughly in running water, especially if buying from unknown sources
:::

## ⚕️ Special Dietary Considerations

### ✅ Safe for Pregnancy (Low Mercury)
Pomfret, Sardines, Anchovies, Rohu, Catla, Tilapia, Pearl Spot, Sole Fish, Indian Salmon, Grouper, Red Snapper, Romeo Fish, Pink/White Perch, White Snapper, Sea Bass, Croaker, Rabbit Fish, all Carp varieties, Murrel, Catfish, Climbing Perch, Wallago, Mystus, Basa, Pangasius, Roop Chand, Bhetki, Mullet, Emperor Fish, Travelly, Golden Anchovy, most Shellfish (when well-cooked)

### ⚠️ Limit During Pregnancy (Moderate Mercury - Max 1-2 servings/week)
Seer Fish, Indian Salmon (Rawas), Tuna (small varieties), Hilsa, Lady Fish, Horse Mackerel, Milk Shark, Eel (limit), Ribbon Fish

### ❌ Avoid During Pregnancy (High Mercury)
Shark (all types), Large Tuna (Yellowfin, Bigeye), Marlin, Sailfish, Barracuda (large), Stingray, Swordfish

### 👶 Good for Children
Pomfret (boneless), Basa, Sea Bass, Tilapia, Murrel (boneless), Sole Fish, Rohu, Catla, Salmon, Anchovies (small), Sardines

### 🏥 Post-Surgery/Recovery
Murrel (top choice), Catfish, Rohu, Eel, Climbing Perch, Snakehead

### ❤️ Heart Patients (High Omega-3)
Mackerel, Sardines, Indian Salmon, Hilsa, Seer Fish (limit), Anchovies, Indian Oil Sardine, Horse Mackerel

### 💪 Bodybuilding/High Protein
Tuna, Seer Fish, Murrel, Cobia, Lobster, Tiger Prawns, Grouper, Emperor Fish, Yellowfin Tuna (limit)
